Financial Literacy · Lesson 1

What is financial literacy?

Financial literacy is simply knowing how to handle money well — how to earn it, spend it, save it, and grow it, without letting it stress you out or control you.

It's not about being rich. Plenty of people who earn a lot of money still struggle, because no one ever taught them how money actually works. Financial literacy is the set of skills that makes that struggle avoidable.

Why it matters, starting now

Most adults say they wish they'd learned about money earlier. The habits you build as a teen — how you think about spending, saving, and debt — tend to stick for life. Starting at 13–18 means you get a head start that most people never get.

The five building blocks

Everything in personal finance comes back to five basic skills:

  • Earning — understanding where money comes from (a job, allowance, a side hustle) and what it's really worth.
  • Spending — telling the difference between a need and a want, and spending on purpose instead of by accident.
  • Saving — setting money aside before you spend the rest, so future-you has options.
  • Investing — putting money to work so it grows over time, instead of just sitting still.
  • Borrowing — understanding debt and credit, so it works for you instead of against you.
Quick example Two people each save $20 a month starting at age 15. One keeps it in cash under a mattress. The other invests it and earns an average 7% a year. By age 65, the difference is well over $100,000 — from the exact same habit, just one extra step: understanding how money grows.

What's next

In the lessons ahead, you'll go through each building block one at a time — starting with budgeting, so you always know where your money is actually going.
